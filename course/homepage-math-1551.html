    Subsection Common Course Questions

    Q: When should I come to office hours?

    A: Anytime you have a question or something you want to talk about! We would especially like you to ask for help (from us or one of your other resources) in the following circumstances, as they’re early indications that the course may not be going in a good direction for you:
    🔗
    • You’re confused about something we did in lecture.
      🔗
      🔗
    • You don’t understand how to do some of the homework (after giving it an honest attempt).
      🔗
      🔗
    • You received a quiz or exam grade that’s lower than the final grade you hope to earn.
      🔗
      🔗
    • There is something going on in your life that has the potential to severely impact your class performance—we don’t need to know the details, but we have much more ability to help you if you reach out before you’re behind or have done poorly on assessments.
      🔗
      🔗
    Reaching out early can keep your challenges from compounding, and can make you much more successful in this course.
    🔗
    🔗

    Q: How do I prepare for exams?

    A: To prepare for exams, students are expected to thoroughly review the course materials, including lecture notes, WeBWork solutions, quiz solutions, practice problems from non-studio weeks, and studio worksheets (with available answer keys and hints), before working through problems from past exams.
    🔗
    🔗

    Q: How do I receive feedback on my quizzes and exams?

    A: Quizzes and exams will be graded electronically and returned to you via Gradescope. Once in Gradescope, you can see which rubric items were applied to your submission and which points you earned by clicking the question number in the column on the right to expand.
    🔗
    🔗

    Q: I’m going to miss a class or assignment. What should I do?

    A: That depends on what you’re going to miss and why:
    🔗
    • Lecture: Lecture attendance is not mandatory but strongly encouraged.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Studio/Quiz: Studio attendance is required (because these sessions are REALLY helpful!). There are 11 studio meetings throughout the semester. Students are allowed to miss up to three studios, regardless of the reason; in other words, the three lowest quiz scores will be dropped. If you have extenuating circumstances that require additional absences beyond this generous buffer, please reach out individually to your instructor or the course coordinator. Absences not discussed by 1 week after the absence may not be excused, even if they were for a good reason.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Exam: Please fill out the Exam Makeup Request Form (linked on Canvas) by the Wednesday before the exam for a planned absence, and as soon as possible (no later than noon the following Monday) otherwise. There will be common makeup exams from 11:00 am to 12:15 pm each Tuesday following an exam for students who must miss the exam for an excused reason. Note that you may not be allowed to make up an exam if you fail to provide timely notification and documentation of your excused absence.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Q: How is my grade calculated?

    A: This is written clearly in the syllabus—please look there for calculations and all related policies.
    🔗
    Canvas will NOT show your current/projected total grade at any point, since there’s no way to program it to take the better of two options as described in the syllabus while also accounting for the WeBWork extra points. However, you can calculate your current grade by following the instructions to enter your current/projected assignment grades into the 1551 Grade Approximator.xlsx spreadsheet on Canvas.
    🔗
    Please note: This calculator is to be used for estimation purposes only, and the calculator and its outputs should not be taken as a guarantee that you will receive a particular grade in the course. You will need to download the calculator in order to use it. Canvas does NOT accurately reflect quiz or midterm or final exam averages, because any missed assignments are recorded as “0,” and Canvas includes these in the calculation.
    🔗
    🔗

    Q: Is there extra credit? Do we drop (quizzes, exams, etc.) in this course? How can I improve my grade?

    A: There is NO extra credit in this course beyond the weekly review sessions and the CIOS incentive offered in the syllabus, either individual or collective. We drop 3 studio participation quizzes.
    🔗
    There are no retakes or opportunities to change your grade on a correctly graded assignment (though we do encourage you to learn from your mistakes). If there are circumstances in your life that you feel will affect an upcoming quiz or exam, please reach out to your instructor beforehand—we want to help, but can’t do so if you go ahead and take the assessment.
    🔗
    The best way to improve your grade is by developing your study skills, strengthening your understanding of the concepts, and preparing effectively for exams. We are here to support you—please seek help whenever you need it.
    🔗
    🔗

    Q: When/where is our exam?

    A: This semester, we will have 3 common exams during the exam period on Friday evenings, from 5:00 pm to 6:15 pm. You can find the dates in our calendar, and should note them at the beginning of the semester so you can plan accordingly and notify your instructor as soon as possible if you will be absent (see above).
    🔗
    Note that there will be no studio on exam days. Please check your final exam schedule carefully, and let us know about any conflicts with at least 2 weeks’ notice. We will provide you with reminders that exams are coming up, but it is ultimately your responsibility to keep informed about the course schedule and prepare for your exams.
    🔗
    Note: Students with extended time accommodations should refer to the question below: “Q: I have accommodations for this course. How do I use them?”
    🔗
    🔗

    Q: Can I use a calculator?

    A: For quizzes and exams, no. We will write problems where numbers work out nicely though, so don’t worry about it. You may run into some messier numbers on your WeBWork homework or studio worksheets, so using a calculator there is just fine (just make sure you’re learning the process too so you won’t be lost without it on the exams).
    🔗
    Please note that WeBWork does not accept decimal answers. Keep your answers as they are (i.e., in rational form, as roots, etc.).
    🔗
    🔗

    Q: I have accommodations for this course. How do I use them?

    A: First, please reach out to your instructor as soon as possible in order to set up a plan and discuss what help you may need from us.
    🔗
    • For studio participation quizzes: You can ask your TA if they’re willing to stay a few minutes (typically at most 5 minutes) after class to provide you your full allotment of time. Please note you must make this request in advance, and your TA is not required to accommodate you. If the TA is unavailable to accommodate your request or if you have any questions, please contact your instructor.
      🔗
      🔗
    • For midterm exams:
      🔗
      • Please schedule your midterm exam with extended time at the Testing Center on the same day as the class exam and at a time as close as possible to the scheduled exam time. For example, for Friday exams, if you have a 1.5X time accommodation, you should plan to begin at 2:45 pm.
        🔗
        🔗
      • If that is not feasible, please contact your instructor to obtain approval to schedule your exams on the Friday of the exam week, during the Testing Center’s business hours, at a time that best fits your schedule. Please let the Testing Center know that we have approved this flexible timing.
        🔗
        🔗
      • If you need to take a makeup exam, you must first obtain permission from your instructor or the course coordinator. Makeup exams should be scheduled at the Testing Center on Tuesday, the designated day for common makeup exams, beginning as close to 11:00 am as possible.
        🔗
        🔗
      • ODS Policy: “Students must schedule exams (tests, quizzes, and midterms) at least five business days in advance. Requests must be submitted through AIM. Please note: weekends are not included in business days.”
        🔗
        🔗
      • Important: Due to the 5 business day notice policy, you may want to schedule both the regular Friday and the makeup Tuesday exams as a precaution. After completing the regular exam, please cancel the Tuesday exam no later than the following Monday.
        🔗
        🔗
      🔗
    • For the final exam:
      🔗
      • Please schedule your final exam at the Testing Center as soon as possible.
        🔗
        🔗
      • ODS Policy: “Students must schedule final exams through AIM at least 30 business days in advance. The available blocks for scheduling final exams are 8 a.m., 11:30 a.m., and 2:45 p.m. Students may not schedule final exams that will occur during reading periods.”
        🔗
        🔗
      🔗
    Please note:
    🔗
    1. You must take care to abide by the testing center’s policies and scheduling deadlines in order to use their services (see Office of Disability Services Scheduling).
      🔗
      🔗
    2. When scheduling at the testing center, you should schedule under your lecture section (i.e., G or M) and NOT your studio section (e.g., G01 or M02). This allows our coordinator to correctly send work to the testing center for you to complete.
      🔗
      🔗
    If you have any other questions or concerns, or need help navigating this process, please reach out to your instructor or the course coordinator.
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Homework

    Q: Where do we complete homework this semester?

    A: We will use the WeBWork system to do our graded online homework—just click the link on the Canvas homepage. You don’t need to “submit” this homework or tell us you’ve completed it—WeBWork automatically saves your best question scores and will update that grade in Canvas, though this syncing may not happen quickly so please don’t panic. Just keep an eye on your grade in WeBWork weekly, and we’ll ask you at the end of the semester to double check that everything looks okay.
    🔗
    If you encounter any technical errors while working on WeBWork, please contact your instructor immediately for guidance. Issues reported after the deadline have passed will not be considered for any reason. Please note that WeBWork does not accept decimal answers. Keep your answers as they are (i.e., in rational form, as roots, etc.).
    🔗
    🔗

    Q: How can I get help on my homework?

    A: You have lots of options! In person, you can stop by your instructor’s office hours, book one-on-one or small group tutoring sessions with TAs, ask a friend, or post questions on Piazza.
    🔗
    For Piazza: Be sure to ask as specific a question as possible (e.g., “Please do this problem for me” is not really a question, and you should include your own thoughts). If applicable, attach a screenshot of your work. Please also scroll through to see if someone has already answered your question before you post!
    🔗
    🔗

    Q: Can I have a homework extension?

    A: No. Homework deadlines are there to keep you on schedule, and you will always have several days between covering the content of a homework set and it being due. These deadlines are firm in all individual circumstances. We offer a number of homework extra points, as detailed in the syllabus, so it is still possible to achieve a perfect score even if you do not fully complete a small number of assignments.
    🔗
    However, if you notice that homework you did has not saved in WeBWork (and this persists for 20 minutes or more), please contact your instructor or the course coordinator immediately.
    🔗
    🔗

    Q: Are there more practice problems I can do?

    A: Always! It’s often effective to search for problems on the topic we’re working on since Calculus is widely studied. Some specific resources we like are:
    🔗
    • Examples and exercises from the textbook: An older version of the textbook is available under Day 1 in the “Lecture and Studio Materials” section.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Paul’s Online Math Notes: This site is essentially a free textbook, with brief explanations and some good practice problems with nice solutions.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Optional MyMathLab Course: We also have set up an optional MyMathLab course, titled “Differential Calculus - MATH-1551 - A/C/F/G/H/HP/K/M”, which includes an electronic copy of our textbook and some more problems in an online homework system—find more information on the MyMathLab Information page on Canvas.
      🔗
      🔗
